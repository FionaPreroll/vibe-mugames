# Changelog

All changes players can notice are listed here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The Spielekiste: board games for phones side by side, connected directly by QR code, without internet or a server. One phone opens a table, the others sit down at it.
- Tic Tac Toe and Four in a Row (_Vier in einer Reihe_), for two players. Everyone else at the table watches, and after a game the winner stays and the loser goes to the back of the queue. Two players take turns in starting.
- Scores for every player at the table, kept while a player drops out and comes back.
- Text codes for phones without a camera.
- English and German.
- Nine Men's Morris (_Mühle_): place nine pieces each, then move them along the lines; a mill takes one of the other player's pieces, and with three left a player's pieces jump.
- Draughts (_Dame_) on 8 by 8 squares: taking is a must, pieces jump on while they can, forward and backward, and crowned pieces fly across the board. Each player sees their own pieces at the bottom.
- Moves of several steps are picked on the board a cell at a time: the piece, then where it goes. The board shows which pieces can move, where they can go, and the move made last.
- Miau-Miau, the card game Mau-Mau, for two to six players, each holding cards only they see. Play a card of the same suit or rank; a seven makes the next player draw two (or answer with a seven), an eight makes them miss a turn, and a jack goes on anything and wishes for a suit. The first to play their last card wins. Up to four players use 32 cards, five and six a full deck of 52.
- Hints for learning Miau-Miau: sevens, eights and jacks carry a mark, a card help explains what each card does, and a line says what the last card does now, such as who must draw or who misses a turn.
- After a game of more than two, the winner stays and the others go to the back of the queue; when nobody waits, the next player in turn starts.
- An Android app, whose newest build is always on the repository's releases page.
- Updates in the Android app, if you agree at its first start or switch them on under _Updates_ on the start screen: at every start, the app looks for a newer build and offers to install it, and _Look for a new version now_ looks at once. The dialog says how large the download is, and warns on mobile data or another metered connection. The download can be paused and goes on where it stopped, also after the app was closed. Android asks before installing; the first time, it wants you to allow the app to install apps, and installing goes on by itself when you come back. If Android's installer does not open, or the update was not installed, the dialog says so and offers to try again.
- The website tells you on the start screen when a newer version is out, and loads it with a tap.

- Play on one device: **Play on this device** on the start screen seats two to six players around one laptop or tablet, without a second phone or any network. Take turns on one screen, which says whose turn it is, or split the screen into a part for each of two to four players, each with their own view, their own cards and, in Draughts, their own pieces at the bottom. A part can be turned round for a player across the table. The device remembers who played and how.
- Miau-Miau on one device is played with open cards, as everyone there can see every hand: the lobby and the game say so.
- Help for cameras that will not focus on the other phone's screen, as in Spark Hunt: tap the picture to focus on that spot; zoom and brightness sliders where the camera has them; **Switch camera** for phones with more than one; and **Take a photo instead**, with the phone's own camera app. The camera last used, its zoom and brightness are remembered.
- Sea Battle (_Schiffe versenken_), for two: each player places a fleet of five ships on a sea of 10 by 10, hidden from the other, then both shoot at each other's sea a square at a time. A hit shoots again; whoever sinks the whole other fleet first wins. Ships may not touch, so the squares around a sunk ship are marked as water. Fleets start shuffled: tap a ship to pick it, tap it again to turn it, and tap the sea to move it. Each phone sees only its own fleet, until the game is over.
- Sea Battle on one device keeps the fleets hidden: taking turns on one screen, the device asks to be handed over between the players and shows nothing until the next one says they hold it; on a split screen, each fleet shows only while its player asks to see it.
- Mäxchen, the dice game of bluffing, for two to eight: roll two dice under the cup, look alone, and say what they show, or bluff, but always more than the player before. The next player believes it and rolls, or doubts it and lifts the cup: whoever was wrong loses one of three lives. Doubles beat the rest, and the Mäxchen, 2-1, beats everything. The last one with a life left wins. Only the roller's phone shows the dice; the game says how often two dice reach what was said, and explains how the throws rank.
- Werewolf (_Werwolf_), the party game of a village with werewolves hidden in it, for four to eight. Each phone shows only its own role: werewolf, seer, healer or villager. At night everyone taps someone in secret: the werewolves a victim, the seer whom to look at, the healer whom to protect, and villagers whom they suspect, so that nobody gives their role away. By day, everyone votes whom to banish, and the role of whoever is out is shown. The village wins once every werewolf is out, the werewolves once they are as many as the others; the win counts for the whole team.
- Mäxchen and Werewolf on one device stay hidden as Sea Battle does: taking turns, the device is handed round, and on a split screen, each player's dice or role shows only while they ask to see it.
- Light Racers (_Lichtrenner_), the first game in real time, for two to six: every rider races across the grid leaving a wall of light behind, and whoever rides into a wall, the edge or another rider is out. The last one riding wins. Turn by swiping on the field, with the ⟲ and ⟳ buttons, or with the arrow keys; a ring shows you your rider before the start.
- Air Hockey, in real time, for two, across two phones laid top edge to top edge: each screen is its player's half of the rink, and the puck slides from one phone to the other. Move your mallet with a finger; the first to seven goals wins. Anyone else at the table watches the whole rink, and the host can stop the game.
- The games in real time on one device: on one screen, each player steers with their own keys (W A S D, the arrow keys, I J K L, the number pad) or buttons, and in Air Hockey with a finger on their half of the rink; on a split screen, each player gets their own field, and in Air Hockey their own half, turned their way.
- Dots and Boxes (_Käsekästchen_), for two: draw lines between the dots in turn; whoever draws the fourth side of a box wins it and draws again. Most of the 25 boxes wins.
- Reversi, for two: place a piece so that it encloses a line of the other's pieces, which turn to your colour. A player who cannot move passes; once neither can, more pieces win. The board marks where you can place a piece, and says when a player had to pass.
- Chinese Checkers (_Sternhalma_), for two to six on a star of 121 holes: bring all ten pieces into the triangle across, by steps and by jumps over any pieces, as many in a row as you like. Pick a piece and tap where it goes, however many jumps away. Each phone turns the star so that its own pieces start at the bottom, and each player's goal is ringed in their colour.
- Ludo, for two to four players, with four pieces each: roll the die, and move a piece that many squares round the board and into your home squares. A six brings a piece out of your corner and rolls again, landing on another player's piece sends it back to its corner, and with no piece out you get three tries for a six. The first with all four pieces home wins. Tap a piece to see where it goes, then tap there; hints say what was rolled and what to do, and _How Ludo goes_ explains the rules. Each phone shows its own corner at the bottom left; it also plays on one device, taking turns or on a split screen.
- Rules for every game, with drawings: the **?** on each game in the chooser, or **Rules** beside the game's name while it is played, explains the game a step at a time, in English and German. Players can read them while the host chooses a game.
- The game chooser shows a small picture of each game's board.

### Changed

- The Android app has an icon of its own instead of Capacitor's: an open games box with a card, a draughts piece and a pawn, on a dark blue board. Android 13 and newer can tint it to the wallpaper's colours, and the app starts on the same box and blue. The website's tab shows the same box.
- Every build of the Android app is now signed with the same key, so a new one installs over the one before and keeps the app's settings. An app installed before this change has to be uninstalled once.

### Fixed

- Pairing by copied text codes works even when sending a code over takes minutes: the joining phone now waits up to 10 minutes for the host, instead of giving up after 30 seconds.
- Pairing says what is going on at every step: that a code was read, that the joining phone waits for the host, and why a connection failed, with **Try again** on both phones. **Copy** always says whether it worked, and a wrong code pasted earlier no longer leaves its message behind.
