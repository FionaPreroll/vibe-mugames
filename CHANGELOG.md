# Changelog

All changes players can notice are listed here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The Spielekiste: board games for phones side by side, connected directly by QR code, without internet or a server. One phone opens a table, the others sit down at it.
- Tic Tac Toe and Four in a Row (_Vier in einer Reihe_), for two players. Everyone else at the table watches, and after a game the winner stays and the loser goes to the back of the queue. Two players take turns in starting.
- Scores for every player at the table, kept while a player drops out and comes back.
- Text codes for phones without a camera.
- English and German.
- Nine Men's Morris (_Mühle_): place nine pieces each, then move them along the lines; a mill takes one of the other player's pieces, and with three left a player's pieces jump.
- Draughts (_Dame_) on 8 by 8 squares: taking is a must, pieces jump on while they can, forward and backward, and crowned pieces fly across the board. Each player sees their own pieces at the bottom.
- Moves of several steps are picked on the board a cell at a time: the piece, then where it goes. The board shows which pieces can move, where they can go, and the move made last.
- Miau-Miau, the card game Mau-Mau, for two to six players, each holding cards only they see. Play a card of the same suit or rank; a seven makes the next player draw two (or answer with a seven), an eight makes them miss a turn, and a jack goes on anything and wishes for a suit. The first to play their last card wins. Up to four players use 32 cards, five and six a full deck of 52.
- Hints for learning Miau-Miau: sevens, eights and jacks carry a mark, a card help explains what each card does, and a line says what the last card does now, such as who must draw or who misses a turn.
- After a game of more than two, the winner stays and the others go to the back of the queue; when nobody waits, the next player in turn starts.
- An Android app, whose newest build is always on the repository's releases page.
- Updates in the Android app, if you agree at its first start or switch them on in the settings: at every start, the app looks for a newer build and offers to install it, and _Look for a new version now_ looks at once. The dialog says how large the download is, and warns on mobile data or another metered connection. The download can be paused and goes on where it stopped, also after the app was closed. Android asks before installing; the first time, it wants you to allow the app to install apps, and installing goes on by itself when you come back. If Android's installer does not open, or the update was not installed, the dialog says so and offers to try again.
- The website can tell you on the start screen when a newer version is out, and loads it with a tap. It is off until you switch it on in the settings.
- The website can be installed from the browser, such as with _Install app_ or _Add to home screen_ in Chrome, and then opens in a window of its own, with the Spielekiste's icon. After one visit it also starts without internet, such as on a hotspot. A newer version is fetched in the background and runs from the next start on, never during a game, or at once with _Load it_. The privacy policy says what the browser keeps for this and when it asks GitHub for a newer version.
- Settings, behind ⚙ on the start screen: the update switches, and _Look for a new version now_, in the app and on the website.
- A link to the Android app on the website's start screen and in its settings.

- Play on one device: **Play on this device** on the start screen seats two to six players around one laptop or tablet, without a second phone or any network. Take turns on one screen, which says whose turn it is, or split the screen into a part for each of two to four players, each with their own view, their own cards and, in Draughts, their own pieces at the bottom. A part can be turned round for a player across the table. The device remembers who played and how.
- Miau-Miau on one device is played with open cards, as everyone there can see every hand: the lobby and the game say so.
- Help for cameras that will not focus on the other phone's screen, as in Spark Hunt: tap the picture to focus on that spot; zoom and brightness sliders where the camera has them; **Switch camera** for phones with more than one; and **Take a photo instead**, with the phone's own camera app. The camera last used, its zoom and brightness are remembered.
- Sea Battle (_Schiffe versenken_), for two: each player places a fleet of five ships on a sea of 10 by 10, hidden from the other, then both shoot at each other's sea a square at a time. A hit shoots again; whoever sinks the whole other fleet first wins. Ships may not touch, so the squares around a sunk ship are marked as water. Fleets start shuffled: tap a ship to pick it, tap it again to turn it, and tap the sea to move it. Each phone sees only its own fleet, until the game is over.
- Sea Battle on one device keeps the fleets hidden: taking turns on one screen, the device asks to be handed over between the players and shows nothing until the next one says they hold it; on a split screen, each fleet shows only while its player asks to see it.
- Mäxchen, the dice game of bluffing, for two to eight: roll two dice under the cup, look alone, and say what they show, or bluff, but always more than the player before. The next player believes it and rolls, or doubts it and lifts the cup: whoever was wrong loses one of three lives. Doubles beat the rest, and the Mäxchen, 2-1, beats everything. The last one with a life left wins. Only the roller's phone shows the dice; the game says how often two dice reach what was said, and explains how the throws rank.
- Werewolf (_Werwolf_), the party game of a village with werewolves hidden in it, for four to eight. Each phone shows only its own role: werewolf, seer, healer or villager. At night everyone taps someone in secret: the werewolves a victim, the seer whom to look at, the healer whom to protect, and villagers whom they suspect, so that nobody gives their role away. By day, everyone votes whom to banish, and the role of whoever is out is shown. The village wins once every werewolf is out, the werewolves once they are as many as the others; the win counts for the whole team.
- Mäxchen and Werewolf on one device stay hidden as Sea Battle does: taking turns, the device is handed round, and on a split screen, each player's dice or role shows only while they ask to see it.
- Light Racers (_Lichtrenner_), the first game in real time, for two to six: every rider races across the grid leaving a wall of light behind, and whoever rides into a wall, the edge or another rider is out. The last one riding wins. Turn by swiping on the field, with the ⟲ and ⟳ buttons, or with the arrow keys; a ring shows you your rider before the start.
- Air Hockey, in real time, for two, across two phones laid top edge to top edge: each screen is its player's half of the rink, and the puck slides from one phone to the other. Move your mallet with a finger; the first to seven goals wins. Anyone else at the table watches the whole rink, and the host can stop the game.
- The games in real time on one device: on one screen, each player steers with their own keys (W A S D, the arrow keys, I J K L, the number pad) or buttons, and in Air Hockey with a finger on their half of the rink; on a split screen, each player gets their own field, and in Air Hockey their own half, turned their way.
- Dots and Boxes (_Käsekästchen_), for two: draw lines between the dots in turn; whoever draws the fourth side of a box wins it and draws again. Most of the 25 boxes wins.
- Reversi, for two: place a piece so that it encloses a line of the other's pieces, which turn to your colour. A player who cannot move passes; once neither can, more pieces win. The board marks where you can place a piece, and says when a player had to pass.
- Chinese Checkers (_Sternhalma_), for two to six on a star of 121 holes: bring all ten pieces into the triangle across, by steps and by jumps over any pieces, as many in a row as you like. Pick a piece and tap where it goes, however many jumps away. Each phone turns the star so that its own pieces start at the bottom, and each player's goal is ringed in their colour.
- Ludo, for two to four players, with four pieces each: roll the die, and move a piece that many squares round the board and into your home squares. A six brings a piece out of your corner and rolls again, landing on another player's piece sends it back to its corner, and with no piece out you get three tries for a six. The first with all four pieces home wins. Tap a piece to see where it goes, then tap there; hints say what was rolled and what to do, and _How Ludo goes_ explains the rules. Each phone shows its own corner at the bottom left; it also plays on one device, taking turns or on a split screen.
- Rules for every game, with drawings: the **?** on each game in the chooser, or **Rules** beside the game's name while it is played, explains the game a step at a time, in English and German. Players can read them while the host chooses a game.
- The game chooser shows a small picture of each game's board.
- **About the Spielekiste,** at the bottom of the start screen: the version and build with its date, the contact address, the source code, what changed from version to version, and the open-source libraries inside with their licenses.
- A privacy policy, in English and German, under _About the Spielekiste_ and on the website at [fionapreroll.github.io/vibe-mugames/privacy.html](https://fionapreroll.github.io/vibe-mugames/privacy.html): no accounts, no server, no advertising and no tracking, and what stays on your device and what other phones see.
- Bluetooth in the Android app: when joining a table or adding a player, choose QR code or Bluetooth. Over Bluetooth, no Wi-Fi is needed: the host's table is visible to phones nearby, the player taps it, and the host lets them in after comparing the code both phones show. One table can mix both.
- _Smooth out the network_ in the settings, for Air Hockey: the puck and the other mallet glide on at an even pace, however unevenly the host's word arrives. On at first: switch it off to see each state as it comes.
- The connection at a glance: at a table with other phones, the top line shows a small graph of the last half minute's ping and its newest value, green, yellow or red; on the host's phone, the worst of the players'. Tapping it opens _Connection details_: a graph of the last minute, and for each connection its ping, the pings lost, stalls, messages and data each way, and how it is carried: Wi-Fi or Bluetooth, the addresses and protocols on both ends, the round trip, packets, bytes, encryption and channels. Addresses stay hidden until you tap _Show addresses_.
- A connection test for the host: _Test connections_ under _Connection details_ sends as much as the connections take for five seconds, beside the game, and shows for every player the ping under load and the kilobytes a second each way, with what was lost. Players see that a test runs, and their own result.
- A debug report, to send when something does not work: _Create debug report_ in the settings, or _Debug report_ under _Connection details_. It says what device, browser and versions this is, the settings, how well the connections of the last table kept up second by second, the last connection test, and what happened recently, but no names, codes or addresses. Read it, then copy it, download it, or open an issue on GitHub with it. The privacy policy says what it holds.
- The screen stays on while you sit at a table, in the app and in browsers that can, so that a phone no longer drops out of the game when its screen goes dark. The privacy policy says so.
- A short buzz when it is your turn, on phones that can; _Buzz when it is my turn_ in the settings switches it off. The app asks Android for vibration, which needs no prompt, and the privacy policy says so.
- A full-screen button (⛶) at the table and when playing on one device, in browsers that can, such as on a tablet or a laptop.
- _Played lately_ on top of the game chooser: the three games chosen last on this device, also still in their own group. The privacy policy says the device keeps them.
- Themes, under _Settings_ → _Theme_: Classic, Light, Black, AMOLED black, Wood, Green felt, Forest, Nord, Gruvbox, Neon, High contrast, Night mode in red, and the seasonal Chinese New Year, Spring, Easter, Summer, Halloween, Winter, Christmas and New Year, with lanterns, cherry blossom, painted eggs, the sun, bats round the moon, falling snow, fairy lights and fireworks behind the screens. They colour everything, the boards, cards, dice and the games in real time too. _With the seasons_, the first choice, changes by itself through the year: winter from November to February, spring from March, summer from June, and the feasts in their days. The settings show each theme as a small picture of itself, and the one picked applies at once. The privacy policy says the device keeps the theme chosen.
- Stickers on the box on the start screen, in their flags' colours, sparkling a little: Trans Day of Visibility, IDAHOBIT, Pride Month, International Non-Binary People's Day, Bi Visibility Day, Coming Out Day, Intersex Awareness Day, Ace Week, Trans Awareness Month and Week, and the Transgender Day of Remembrance, whose sticker holds a quietly glowing candle instead. When several fall together, each has its own. Hover over a sticker or tap it to read what it is for.
- Ultimate Tic Tac Toe, for two: nine boards of Tic Tac Toe make one big board, and the square you mark sends the other player to the small board in the same place. Win three small boards in a row to win. The board marks where the next mark must go, and below it counts the boards each player has won.
- Schwimmen (also called 31), the card game for two to eight: swap cards with the three in the middle to get as many points of one suit as you can, up to 31, and knock when your hand is good. The fewest points cost a coin; without one left you swim, and the last one still in wins. Each phone sees only its own hand; on one device, the device is handed over, or on a split screen each hand stays hidden until its player shows it. A help explains what the cards count, a line says what the player before did, and after each round everyone's hands are shown.
- City, Country, River (_Stadt, Land, Fluss_), for two to eight: each round has a letter, and everyone writes a city, a country, a river, a first name, an animal and a job that start with it, each on their own phone. Whoever has all six may call Stop, and the others have ten seconds left; after three minutes, the round ends anyway. Then everyone sees every answer and taps those that do not count: struck by more than half of the others, they count nothing. An answer alone in its category counts 20, one nobody else has 10, one shared 5; after five rounds the most points win. On one device, everyone writes in turn while the others look away, and the answers are checked together.
- Draw and Guess (_Montagsmaler_), for two to eight: in turn, each player chooses one of three words and draws it on their phone, while the others watch the drawing grow on theirs and type their guesses. Who gets it first scores 3, the next 2, everyone after 1, and the one drawing 1 for each who got it; with half of the time gone a letter shows, and another near the end. A guess that is nearly right tells only its writer so. Draw with five inks and an eraser, undo a stroke or clear the paper; a word said aloud counts too, when the one drawing taps who said it. Everyone draws twice with up to three players, once with more. On one device, the one drawing chooses their word alone, then everyone watches and calls out, and the one drawing taps who got it.
- Who Am I? (_Wer bin ich?_), for two to eight: everyone gets a word, an animal, a job, a figure from a fairy tale or a thing, which all the others see on their phones but they do not, only what kind of thing it is. In turn, ask a question that can be answered with yes or no; the others tap Yes, No or Not sure within 45 seconds, and the most answers decide. After a no, the next player asks. Type a guess and tap _That's me!_: the right word, in German or English, gets it at once, anything else the others judge. Pass, or give up to see your word. Once all but one know theirs, the first to know wins. On one device, everyone asks and answers aloud: only the word of the one asking shows, once they look away, and they tap what the others said.
- A new group in the game chooser, Party games, for games of words, drawing and guessing.
- Snakes (_Schlangen_), in real time, for two to six on one grid: every snake crawls on by itself and grows by one with each apple it eats. Crawling into the edge, into itself or into another snake puts a snake out, and frees the cells it lay on. The first snake 15 long wins, or the last one still crawling. Steer as in Light Racers: swipe on the field, tap ⟲ and ⟳, or use the arrow keys; above the field, how long each snake is.
- Reaction (_Reaktion_), in real time, for two to six: everyone waits until the field lights up, then taps it as fast as they can, and whoever is fastest scores. Tapping before the signal is too early and scores nothing. Each phone measures its own player's time, from the moment it shows the signal, so a slow connection costs nobody a point. The first to five points wins, if nobody else has as many.
- Colour Echo (_Farbecho_), a memory game in real time, for two to six: a pad of four colours lights up a row, then everyone taps it back on their own pad, all at once. A wrong colour, or five seconds without a tap, puts a player out; everyone still in gets the same row again, one colour longer and a little faster. The last one in wins once they have tapped their row back; if the last ones go out in the same row, whoever got further in it wins.
- Snakes, Reaction and Colour Echo on one device: on one screen, each player steers with their own keys (W A S D, the arrow keys, I J K L, the number pad) and, in Reaction and Colour Echo, has a button or a pad of their own; on a split screen, each part has its own field.

### Changed

- The connection details and the debug report say whether a Bluetooth table is still on Bluetooth, or Nearby moved it up to Wi-Fi by itself, and how much it carries. The debug report also says how many messages the Bluetooth plugin dropped.
- _Smooth out the network_ is on now, also on phones where it was off: it makes Air Hockey over Bluetooth or a busy Wi-Fi feel far less jerky. It can still be switched off in the settings.
- Over Bluetooth, players of Air Hockey and Light Racers get every state of the game, 30 a second instead of 15, so the puck and the other players show sooner and closer to where they are.
- On the Air Hockey rink, which covers the screen, the small graph of the ping and its newest value show beside the score, as they do in the top line in other games. Tapping them opens the connection details.
- Air Hockey shows each player the whole rink from their end, with their goal at the bottom. Their own half alone, for two phones laid top edge to top edge, is a setting now: _Show the whole rink_.
- Phones with this version and the version before cannot play together: update both.
- The Android app has an icon of its own instead of Capacitor's: an open games box with a card, a draughts piece and a pawn, on a dark blue board. Android 13 and newer can tint it to the wallpaper's colours, and the app starts on the same box and blue. The website's tab shows the same box.
- Every build of the Android app is now signed with the same key, so a new one installs over the one before and keeps the app's settings. An app installed before this change has to be uninstalled once.
- A new start screen: a large picture of the Spielekiste, an open box with boards, cards, dice and pieces, and a line that says it is for everyone at one table, on phones side by side or around one tablet or laptop. On a wide screen, the picture stands beside the buttons.
- The game chooser sorts the games into _Tactics_, _Luck and bluff_ and _Skill_ (the games in real time), and each game says how many play it. On a laptop or tablet, the chooser uses the whole width.
- On a laptop, a tablet on its side or a phone on its side, a game shows its board beside whose turn it is and the players, as large as the screen is high, so nothing needs scrolling. A tablet held upright shows larger boards too.
- When a game starts, the screen begins at its top, also when the chooser was scrolled down.

### Fixed

- At a table over Bluetooth that Nearby moved up to Wi-Fi, the connection test measures the connection again: before, the data from players was dropped whole on the way, and half the test's pings went missing, while the game itself came through. Such a table also has more room for the game's messages.
- The host's connection graph no longer turns red, and the log no longer notes a stall, for the second between the pings of a player who is waiting in the lobby or at a board game, nor right after a player joined.
- In Air Hockey, your own mallet follows your finger at once, also without _Smooth out the network_, instead of waiting for the host's answer. Over Bluetooth that wait felt like lag: a tenth to a quarter of a second.
- At a table on a narrow phone, the top line no longer sticks out past the right edge: the connection takes the title's place, and _Add player_ fits beside it.
- A name typed on the start screen stays when you visit the settings or change the language, instead of being forgotten.
- A Bluetooth player the host let in whose phone never connects no longer stays on _Connecting…_ in the host's list: after 30 seconds, the list says the other phone did not answer in time.
- Over a weak Wi-Fi, a player's phone no longer goes back to an older table for a moment when the host's messages arrive out of order.
- Pairing by copied text codes works even when sending a code over takes minutes: the joining phone now waits up to 10 minutes for the host, instead of giving up after 30 seconds.
- Pairing says what is going on at every step: that a code was read, that the joining phone waits for the host, and why a connection failed, with **Try again** on both phones. **Copy** always says whether it worked, and a wrong code pasted earlier no longer leaves its message behind.
